Physical Properties of Mn3O4 Nanoparticles One-Step Synthesized at Room Temperature and Short Time

Frank R. Sánchez-Monteza, Segundo R. Jáuregui-Rosas, Olga V. Soledad Flores
GMIN – Universidad Nacional de Trujillo, Peru · Department of Physics, University of Puerto Rico – Mayagüez
Chemical Engineering Transactions, Vol. 123, pp. 37–42 (2026) · AIDIC · ISSN 2283-9216
Read PDF DOI: 10.3303/CET26123007 Copied!

Summary

We developed a simple, one-step coprecipitation route that produces Mn3O4 nanoparticles at room temperature in only 30 minutes, with no thermal post-treatment. The route reacts MnCl2 with NaOH (0.1–0.3 M) in water or in ethanol. XRD, Raman, and FTIR confirmed a single-phase tetragonal spinel. UV-Vis diffuse reflectance gave band gaps above the bulk value, and photoluminescence revealed several defect-related emission bands. The choice of solvent had a much stronger effect on size and optical properties than the NaOH concentration.

Key results

Single phaseTetragonal hausmannite (I41/amd) with no secondary phases; a = 0.576 nm, c ≈ 0.95–0.96 nm.
Tunable band gapEg = 2.82–2.89 eV (water) and 2.37–2.64 eV (ethanol), compared with 2.30 eV for bulk.
Size control by solventCrystallite size of 21–25 nm (water) and 9–11 nm (ethanol), about 50–60% smaller in ethanol.
Spherical morphologySEM particle sizes of 46–54 nm (water) and 30–33 nm (ethanol).
Vibrational fingerprintRaman Eg, T2g, and A1g modes at ≈ 318, 372, and 658 cm−1.
Defect emissionPL bands at 460–589 nm, related to oxygen vacancies and Mn3+ d–d transitions.
